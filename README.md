# Project Noralet

Project Noralet is an experimental artificial-intelligence research project investigating the relationship between **computation, emergent cognition, and consciousness**.

The project aims to construct artificial agents—referred to as **Noralets**—inside a deliberately controlled experimental environment and observe how increasingly complex internal organization and behavior may emerge from computational processes.

The central question is not assumed to have a predetermined answer:

> **Can an artificial computational system develop properties that give us serious reason to consider the possibility of consciousness?**

A related goal is to explore the apparent boundary between **numbers, computation, cognition, and mind**.

---

## Research Approach

Project Noralet does not begin by assuming that Noralets are conscious.

It also does not begin by assuming artificial consciousness is impossible.

Instead, the project attempts to construct systems whose development can be observed from relatively simple beginnings and study phenomena such as:

* emergent internal representations;
* learning;
* memory;
* communication;
* social behavior;
* representations of other agents;
* persistent preferences;
* self-related representations;
* behavioral adaptation;
* and other structures potentially relevant to cognition.

No individual behavior is automatically considered evidence of consciousness.

The project places strong emphasis on alternative explanations, reproducibility, observability, and preservation of pre-experiment assumptions.

# Repository Documentation

Project documentation is divided into three major domains.

```text
/
├── research-docs/
├── architecture-docs/
└── codex-reports/
```

### `research-docs/`

Contains the conceptual and scientific record of the project:

* foundations;
* research questions;
* hypotheses;
* experiment definitions;
* observations;
* analyses;
* ethics;
* conclusions.

### `architecture-docs/`

Contains the technical design of the experimental system:

* system architecture;
* Noralet architecture;
* world design;
* learning systems;
* observability;
* data structures;
* architectural decisions.

### `codex-reports/`

Contains iteration-level implementation reports generated by Codex.

Example:

```text
codex-reports/operation-report-001.md
```

These reports provide an audit trail of what was implemented, tested, changed, or discovered during development.

# Development Workflow

Project Noralet follows a documentation-driven iterative workflow.

At a high level:

```text
Research discussion
        ↓
Research documentation
        ↓
Architecture discussion
        ↓
Architecture documentation
        ↓
Codex implementation
        ↓
Codex operation report
        ↓
Review
        ↓
Next iteration
```

Research and architecture documents are primarily developed through discussions with ChatGPT.

Codex performs implementation work and produces operation reports describing the resulting changes.

Codex may freely inspect the project's research, architecture, and previous implementation documentation when performing its tasks.

# Repository Principles

Several principles apply throughout the project.

## Preserve the difference between design and implementation

Architecture documents describe intended behavior.

Source code and tests describe current implemented behavior.

These may temporarily differ during active development.

## Preserve research history

Predictions and hypotheses should be recorded before relevant experimental results whenever practical.

Later observations should not silently rewrite earlier expectations.

## Prefer emergence over scripted appearance

Where relevant to the research question, important cognitive structures should not simply be hard-coded merely to produce convincing behavior.

The distinction between deliberately simulated behavior and genuinely emergent organization is central to the project.

## Keep the system observable

The project should aim to make the development of Noralet behavior and internal organization inspectable rather than treating the system purely as a black box.

## Negative results are valid results

The goal of Project Noralet is not to prove artificial consciousness.

Evidence that apparently complex behavior can be explained without consciousness would itself be scientifically valuable.

# Current Status

Project Noralet is currently in its foundational research and design stage.

Core research questions, terminology, experimental assumptions, architecture, and methodology will be documented before major experimental conclusions are drawn.

The project is expected to evolve substantially as these questions are investigated.
